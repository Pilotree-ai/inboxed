# Copy check

Read the draft the user pastes (subject and body) and flag what makes filters or people treat it as spam. Modern filters care far more about links, formatting and how recipients react than about "spam words", so weigh it that way.

## Red flags (3 points each)

- More than one link in a first email, or any link in a first cold email to someone who has never heard of the sender. Recommend no links in step 1.
- A link shortener (bit.ly, tinyurl and similar), or link text that shows a different domain from the real link.
- An attachment, or an image, in a cold first email.
- Heavy HTML: buttons, banners, multiple fonts or colours. Cold email should look like a person typed it.
- No opt-out line, or no physical way to say no. Some laws require one (UK PECR and GDPR, CAN-SPAM in the US).
- A subject in ALL CAPS, or with `!!!`, `$$$`, or `RE:`/`FWD:` on an email that isn't a reply or forward.
- Merge tags left unfilled (`{first_name}`, `[Company]`, `<<name>>`).

## Amber flags (1 point each)

- Over 150 words in a first email. 50 to 125 is the sweet spot.
- No personal detail at all: nothing shows the sender looked at the recipient.
- Pushy phrasing: "act now", "limited time", "guaranteed", "risk-free", "100% free", "no obligation", "click here".
- More than one question or call to action.
- Exclamation marks in the subject.
- Subject over 60 characters.
- Tracking pixels switched on for a brand-new domain (mention only if the user says they track opens).

## How to report it

List each flag with the exact words from the draft, and give a fixed version of only the lines that need changing. Don't rewrite the whole email unless asked. If there are no flags, say so in one line.
